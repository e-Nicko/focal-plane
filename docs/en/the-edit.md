# The edit

Seven shots, hard cuts, sixteen seconds.
No dissolves, no wipes, no camera flights between shots.

## Hard cuts

A cut is instant and costs the viewer little,
provided that something starts to move at the cut.

- **Cut on the start of a movement.**
  Every shot with a cursor sets the cursor moving in its first frames,
  so each cut coincides with an onset of motion,
  and such cuts go unnoticed more often
  (Smith & Henderson, 2008, in [why it works](why-it-works.md)).
- **Let the click motivate the cut.**
  The tabs shot ends as the ripple of the click on Forecast dies out,
  and the next shot opens on the Forecast view.

The film fades in over 0.35 s at its start and fades out over 0.7 s at its end.
There are no other fades.

## A story

Show a question and its answer,
and leave out every widget that does not serve them:

1. **Establish** the page and its headline number (overview).
2. **Act**: pick a view, switch it on, push it further (tabs, forecast, horizon).
3. **Read** what the action produced (months).
4. **Discover** what does not fit (anomaly).
5. **Pull back** over the same page, now changed (outro).

The outro is the opening shot again, with the story's consequences in it:
the forecast on, the 90-day horizon, the outliers ringed.
The viewer recognises the page and sees what moved.

## Rhythm

Shots run from 1.8 to 2.6 seconds.
Steep and calm shots alternate ([the shot](the-shot.md)),
and the one change of side, to the right, comes with a new subject, the months.
The longest shot, anomaly, carries the discovery.

## Continuity

Every shot reads the same data module,
so a number seen in one shot is the same number in the next
([honest data](honest-data.md)).
The page, the type and the grade never change between shots.
Only the camera does.

## The poster frame

A viewer who prefers reduced motion gets a still frame:
5.8 s, the forecast drawn and its value labelled.
Choose the frame that tells the most of the story on its own.

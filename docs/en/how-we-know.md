# How we know

Every claim in these pages is either measured, sourced, or marked as craft.
This page says how.

## The reference was measured

The starting point was an 8.4-second promo of a type catalogue,
3840×2158 at 7.7 Mbit/s.
It is not in this repository:
its rights are unknown, and it was most likely generated by a video model.
We measured it frame by frame:
where the cuts fall, how long the shots last,
the darkest and the brightest pixel values, the saturation.
The numbers in [the look](the-look.md) come from there.

## Names come with sources

The vocabulary of the genre was collected from stock sites, tool vendors and glossaries.
In [sources](../sources.md) every link carries a mark:
🟢 the page was read,
🟡 seen in search results only,
⚪ a claim the source makes that we could not check.
Counts such as "about 3,500 templates" are search results on a stated date
and make no claim about the market.

## Research is cited, craft is labelled

[Why it works](why-it-works.md) separates what perception research supports
from what is only convention,
and says where a finding stops applying.

## Every render is looked at

- Composition is done on stills: render stills at a few times, look, move a camera key, repeat.
- A contact sheet shows the whole edit at once, for rhythm and continuity.
- An MP4 is checked by decoding frames from the file itself.
  A render is not done until its frames have been seen decoded.

## Changes are diffed

When the engine moved into this repository,
stills from the old code and the new were compared pixel by pixel.
Five of seven test frames were identical;
the other two differed only in the brand name in the wordmark, which was changed on purpose.
Then the fonts moved into the repository,
and the remaining differences were two glyphs, ≈ and σ,
which had been drawn by the system's fallback fonts before.
Then the outlier detector stopped being told where the outliers were planted
([honest data](honest-data.md)),
and the headline label changed from +4.0σ to +3.8σ.

Last, the code moved from JavaScript to TypeScript, and the tools from Node and Playwright to Bun.
Nine of ten stills were identical to the earlier ones.
The tenth, a 1920×1080 master frame, differed by one level in 41 of its 2,073,600 pixels.
The same difference appears when Playwright drives the new code,
so the browser driver is not its source,
and the library files are byte-identical to the ones the old page loaded from a CDN.
We did not find the cause.
Repeated runs of the same command differ from one another by about as much:
one level in up to 0.0015% of a frame at 1280×720.
We treat that as GPU noise.

## The skill was tried on a new brief

An agent with only the skill in hand made a two-shot film for a fictional thermostat app.
It needed five rounds of stills for one shot and three for the other,
and reported ten problems in the skill and the kit.
One was a real bug:
a number resting on 19 showed its tens reel turned by 7%.
It is fixed, along with the wrong or missing lines in the skill.
The film itself is not shipped.

## Speed is measured

`bun run timings` fences each pipeline stage with a one-pixel readback
and prints GPU milliseconds.
The numbers in [the pipeline](pipeline.md) come from it, on the stated hardware.

## What we do not know

- Whether the craft rules hold for viewers outside design and tech.
- How much of the effect survives on a phone screen, where the blur is smaller than a pixel in places.
- Whether the reference's author used a generator for all of it or only for parts.
